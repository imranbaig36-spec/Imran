## Welcome

Microsoft 365 Copilot is built right into the apps you already use — Outlook, Word, and Excel. It can save you real time on everyday tasks, but getting useful results depends on how you ask.

This guide gives you a simple formula to follow and ready-to-use examples you can adapt today.

---

## The Good Prompt Formula

> **Task + Context + Format**

| Part | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Task** | What you want Copilot to do | "Summarise", "Draft", "Create a table" |
| **Context** | Relevant background | "from this email thread", "for a monthly finance report" |
| **Format** | How you want the output | "in bullet points", "as a short paragraph", "with three columns" |

**Quick example:**  
*"Draft a reply to this email thanking the supplier for their quote and asking for a revised delivery date. Write it in two short paragraphs."*

See how that covers all three parts? Task (*draft a reply*), Context (*thanking the supplier, asking for a new date*), Format (*two short paragraphs*). You don't need all three every time — but the more detail you give, the better the result.

## Outlook Examples

### 1. Summarise a long email thread
> *"Summarise this email thread in bullet points. Tell me: who the key people are, what decision needs to be made, and what the deadline is."*

Use this when you've come back from leave or been copied into a long chain you don't have time to read in full.

---

### 2. Draft a reply to a difficult email
> *"Draft a polite but firm reply to this email explaining that the requested deadline cannot be met and proposing an alternative date of next Friday. Keep it professional and under 100 words."*

Copilot will write the first version — you review and adjust the tone before sending.

---

### 3. Write a meeting invitation from notes
> *"I need to invite the team to a budget review meeting. Draft a clear meeting invitation for Thursday at 2pm, lasting one hour. The agenda covers Q3 spend, Q4 forecast, and any outstanding approvals. Keep it brief and professional."*

---

## Word Examples

### 1. Turn bullet points into a polished paragraph
> *"I've written these rough notes about our onboarding process. Rewrite them as a clear, professional paragraph suitable for a staff handbook. Keep the meaning the same but improve the flow and grammar."*

Type (or paste) that prompt into the Copilot chat panel, then paste your rough notes underneath it and press Enter. Copilot will treat everything you've written as one request.

---

### 2. Create a first draft from a brief
> *"Write a first draft of a one-page business update for the Finance team covering: the successful system migration last week, a reminder about the new expense process launching Monday, and contact details for support queries. Friendly but professional tone."*

---

### 3. Shorten or simplify a document section
> *"This paragraph is too long for a summary document. Rewrite it in plain English at no more than 60 words, keeping all the key facts."*

Type that prompt into Copilot, then paste the paragraph you want shortened underneath it and press Enter.

---

## Excel Examples

### 1. Write a formula in plain English
> *"I have a column of invoice amounts in column B and a column of VAT rates in column C. Write a formula in column D that calculates the total amount including VAT for each row."*

Copilot will write the formula and explain what each part does.

---

### 2. Explain what an existing formula does
> *"Can you explain what this formula does in plain English? =SUMIFS(D2:D100,B2:B100,"Approved",C2:C100,"Q3")"*

Don't worry — you don't need to understand that formula. That's exactly the point. If you open a spreadsheet and see something baffling, just copy it and ask Copilot to explain it in plain English. It'll tell you what it does and why.

---

### 3. Suggest how to structure a tracker
> *"I need to track supplier invoices for the Finance team. Suggest a simple table structure with column headings that would work well in Excel, including fields for approval status and payment date."*

## A Note on Data Safety — One Simple Habit

Good news: Microsoft 365 Copilot is part of your organisation's Microsoft 365 account — it's not the same as typing something into a public website like ChatGPT. It works within FinBridge's own secure environment.

That said, there's one easy habit worth building wherever you use AI:

**Before you type, ask yourself: does this contain anyone's personal details?**

Things like a client's full name, account number, date of birth, or address don't need to go into a prompt to get a useful result. Keep it general instead — write *"a client"* or *"a supplier"* rather than a real person's name. You'll get just as good an answer, and you've protected that person's information.

> This is the same habit from **Day 1 of this programme**: *"If someone else could read this prompt, would that be a problem?"* If yes, swap the real detail for a general description before pressing Enter.

That's it. One quick check, and you're good to go. Copilot is here to make your day easier — this habit just makes sure it stays safe for everyone.
